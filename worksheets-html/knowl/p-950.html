  • \(\mu\) is the population mean.
  • \(\sigma\) is the population standard deviation.
  • \(n\) is the sample size.
  • \(\overline{x}\) is the sample mean.
  • \(s\) is the sample standard deviation.
  • \(SD(\overline{x})\) is the standard deviation of the statistic.
  • \(SD = \sigma/\sqrt{n} \) is the theory-based standard deviation of the null distribution.
  • \(SE = s / \sqrt{n} \) is the theory-based standard error, which approximates \(SD\) and \(SD(\overline{x})\text{.}\)
  • \(\text{standardized statistic} = \frac{\text{statistic} - \text{mean of the null distribution}}{\text{SD of the null distribution}} \) is the number of standard deviations away from the mean of the sampling distribution a statistic is.
  • \(t = \frac{\overline{x} - \mu}{\text{Simulated SD of null distribution}} \) is the simulation-based standardized statistic for one mean.
  • \(t = \frac{\overline{x} - \mu}{SE} = \frac{\overline{x} - \mu}{s/\sqrt{n}} \) is the theory-based standardized statistic for one mean.
  • \(\text{statistic} \pm \text{margin of error} = \text{statistic} \pm \text{(multiplier)} \times \text{(SD of statistic)}\) is a confidence interval.
  • \(\overline{x} \pm 2 \times SD(\overline{x}) \) is a 2SD CI approximating a 95% confidence interval estimating \(\mu\text{.}\)
  • \(\overline{x} \pm \text{(multiplier)} \times SE \) is a theory-based confidence interval estimating \(\mu\text{,}\) where the multiplier comes from the t-distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom.
