1. Ask a research question.
Confirm the question can be addressed by collecting data. Typical questions involve comparing groups, asking how two characteristics are related, or surveying people’s opinions.
2. Design a study and collect data.
Plan how to select the people or objects to be studied, decide how to gather the relevant data, and carry out the data collection in a careful, systematic manner.
3. Explore the data.
Look for patterns that relate to the research question or raise additional questions.
4. Draw inferences beyond the data.
Determine whether any findings in the data reflect a genuine tendency and estimate the strength of that tendency.
5. Formulate conclusions
Consider whether inferences made in step 4 can be generalized to processes or groups beyond those represented in the study. Identify whether a cause-and-effect conclusion is warranted.
6. Look back and ahead.
Point out limitations of the study and suggest new studies to build on the findings.
