Exercise 4.

To find out how many children we expect to be in the (darkness, nearsighted) cell of the table, answer (a)-(d):

(a)

Find the overall proportion of children who were nearsighted in the sample.

(b)

How many children in the sample slept in darkness?

(c)

Multiply these two numbers together to find the “expected” number of children who would be nearsighted in the “slept in darkness” group if the null hypothesis were true (the probability of nearsightedness is the same in each condition).

(d)

How different is this expected cell count from what was observed for that cell? What does this suggest about the strength of evidence against the null hypothesis?
